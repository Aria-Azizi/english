Future Perfect

The "Completion by a Deadline" Tense

⏳ Timeline & Concept
Past NOW Action Finished Future Deadline Future

"Looking back from a point in the future to see what is already completed."

⚙️ Morphosyntactic Structure
ACTIVE VOICE
(+) Subject + will have + Past Participle (V3)

"By 2050, scientists will have discovered new energy sources."

(-) Subject + will not (won't) have + Past Participle

"They won't have finished the project by next month."

(?) Will + Subject + have + Past Participle?

"Will humanity have colonized Mars by the next century?"

PASSIVE VOICE (Band 8+ Goldmine)
(+) Subject + will have been + Past Participle

"By 2030, the new infrastructure will have been completed."

(-) Subject + will not have been + Past Participle

"The targets won't have been met by the deadline."

(?) Will + Subject + have been + Past Participle?

"Will the funds have been distributed before the winter?"

📝 Spelling & Advanced Mechanics
The Magic Word: "BY"
By = Not later than:

Future Perfect almost ALWAYS requires the word by (or phrases like by the time, before) to set the deadline.

By tomorrow | By next year | By the end of this decade "In" vs "By":

In 2050 -> Simple Future (It will happen during that year).
By 2050 -> Future Perfect (It will be finished before that year begins).

"By the time" + Present Simple
Advanced Time Clauses:

When your deadline is an action instead of a date, you must use Present Simple for the deadline action.

"By the time we arrive (Present Simple), the movie will have started (Future Perfect)."

* Never say: By the time we will arrive.

🛠️ Core Use Cases (When to use it)
1. Completion Before a Future Deadline

اتمام کار قبل از یک ضرب‌الاجل در آینده

The most common use. You focus on the fact that an action will be 100% finished before a specific time.

"I will have finished this report by Friday."
"By 8 PM, we will have eaten dinner."
2. Duration Up to a Future Time

طول مدت یک کار تا یک زمان در آینده

Used with stative verbs (be, know, have) + for/since to show how long a state will have lasted.

"By next month, I will have known her for 5 years."
"Next year, they will have been married for a decade."
3. Deductions & Assumptions (Advanced)

گمانه‌زنی و فرض درباره زمان حال/گذشته

Used to express a strong assumption that something has *already* happened by now.

"Don't call him now. He will have gone to sleep." (I am sure he is asleep already)
"They will have heard the news by now."
🎯 IELTS Strategic Application
Writing Task 1 Mastering Future Projections

When a chart shows projections (e.g., population in 2040, 2050), using Future Perfect Passive to describe the ultimate endpoint is a guaranteed way to boost your Grammatical Range score.

Band 8 Example: "It is predicted that by 2050, the use of fossil fuels will have been almost entirely phased out (Passive), while renewable energy will have become the dominant source."
Writing Task 2 Concluding Paragraphs & Ultimate Outcomes

In your conclusion or when discussing the long-term effects of a problem, use Future Perfect to project what the world will look like if action is (or isn't) taken.

Band 8 Example: "If immediate action is not taken, irreversible damage will have been inflicted upon the environment by the end of this century."
Speaking Part 3 Answering "How will things be different?"

Showcase your fluency by looking ahead to a specific deadline and describing what society will have accomplished by then.

Band 8 Example: "By the time the next generation grows up, I believe medicine will have advanced so much that many of today's diseases will have been eradicated."
🚫 Common Band 6 Pitfalls

Avoid these mistakes to secure a Band 7+ in Grammatical Range and Accuracy (GRA):

  • 1. The "Lonely Future Perfect" Error: You CANNOT use Future Perfect without stating a clear deadline (using 'by' or 'before'). If there is no deadline, just use Simple Future.
    ❌ One day, humanity will have lived on Mars. (No deadline!)
    ✅ One day, humanity will live on Mars. (Simple Future)
    ✅ By 2080, humanity will have lived on Mars for decades. (Correct)
  • 2. Forgetting "been" in the Passive Form: This is the most common mistake in Task 1 writing. When talking about things being built, replaced, or completed, it MUST be passive.
    ❌ By 2030, the new hospital will have built. (The hospital built itself!)
    ✅ By 2030, the new hospital will have been built.
  • 3. Will inside a "By the time" clause: Just like the First Conditional, you cannot use 'will' directly after time words.
    ❌ By the time he will finish his studies, he will have spent $50,000.
    ✅ By the time he finishes his studies, he will have spent $50,000. (Present Simple + Future Perfect)
