Simple Future (Will)

The Tense of Predictions, Promises, and Logical Projections

⏳ Timeline & Concept
Past NOW (Decision/Prediction) Future Action Future

"Used for facts about the future, opinions, and instant decisions made at the moment of speaking."

⚙️ Morphosyntactic Structure
ACTIVE VOICE
(+) Subject + will + Base Verb

"Artificial intelligence will replace many routine jobs."

(-) Subject + will not (won't) + Base Verb

"The new policy will not solve the core issue."

(?) Will + Subject + Base Verb?

"Will renewable energy become the primary source?"

PASSIVE VOICE (Band 8+)
(+) Subject + will be + Past Participle

"A new high-speed railway will be constructed by 2030."

(-) Subject + will not be + Past Participle

"These traditional methods will not be used anymore."

(?) Will + Subject + be + Past Participle?

"Will the funds be allocated to education?"

📝 Advanced Mechanics: Will vs. Going To
Using "WILL" (Opinions & Instant)
Predictions based on OPINION:

What you think or believe will happen (no hard evidence right now).

"I think humanity will colonize Mars one day." Instant Decisions:

Deciding to do something right at the moment you speak.

"It's cold in here. I will close the window."
Using "BE GOING TO" (Evidence & Plans)
Predictions based on EVIDENCE:

You can see or feel it happening right now.

"Look at those black clouds! It is going to rain." (NOT will rain) Prior Plans/Intentions:

Something you decided before speaking.

"I am going to study medicine next year."
🛠️ Core Use Cases (When to use it)
1. Predictions & Beliefs

پیش‌بینی‌ها بر اساس نظر شخصی

Often used with words like 'think', 'believe', 'expect', 'probably'.

"I expect that prices will drop soon."
"He will probably win the election."
2. Conditional (Type 1)

جملات شرطی نوع اول (محتمل)

Used in the "result" clause of a realistic future condition.

"If we invest in solar power, emissions will decrease."
"If I study hard, I will pass the exam."
3. Promises, Offers & Refusals

قول دادن، پیشنهاد دادن یا امتناع

Used in interactions to guarantee something or refuse to do it.

"I promise I will help you tomorrow."
"The car won't start!" (Refusal by an object)
4. Future Facts

حقایق قطعی در آینده

Things that are certain to happen in the future, regardless of our plans.

"The sun will rise at 6:30 AM tomorrow."
"She will be 30 years old next month."
🎯 IELTS Strategic Application
Writing Task 1 Forecasting Future Data (Projections)

When a graph shows years in the future (e.g., predicted population in 2040), you MUST use future tenses. Using Passive Voice here is the ultimate Band 8+ technique.

Band 8 Example: "It is projected that the demand for electricity will soar, and consequently, new power plants will be constructed."
Writing Task 2 Predicting Consequences & Offering Solutions

Use the First Conditional to explain what will happen if we take (or don't take) a specific action regarding a global issue.

If + Present Simple, Subject + will + Base Verb
Band 8 Example: "If governments implement stricter taxation on fast food, public health will likely improve in the long term."
Speaking Part 3 Speculating About the Future

Examiners often ask "How do you think this will change in the future?". Combine 'will' with hedging words (probably, definitely, likely) to show advanced fluency.

Band 8 Example: "I think traditional classrooms will probably become obsolete as virtual reality takes over."
🚫 Common Band 6 Pitfalls

Avoid these mistakes to secure a Band 7+ in Grammatical Range and Accuracy (GRA):

  • 1. The "When I will" Time Clause Error: NEVER use 'will' directly after time words like when, as soon as, before, until, if. You must use Present Simple in the time clause.
    ❌ I will call you when I will arrive at the hotel.
    ✅ I will call you when I arrive at the hotel.
  • 2. Missing "be" in the Passive Voice: A fatal mistake in Writing Task 1 when describing future developments.
    ❌ By 2040, many old buildings will replaced by skyscrapers. (They will replace themselves!)
    ✅ By 2040, many old buildings will be replaced by skyscrapers.
  • 3. Overusing "Will" for Fixed Plans: If you already have a ticket, a booking, or a firm plan, native speakers prefer Present Continuous or "Going to".
    ❌ I will fly to London tomorrow. I have the tickets. (Sounds grammatically stiff)
    ✅ I am flying to London tomorrow. I have the tickets. (Band 8 natural flow)
