Present Perfect Continuous

Mastery of Duration, Persistence, and Unfinished Actions

⏳ Timeline & Concept
Past Action Started Still Happening NOW Future

"Focus on the DURATION of the activity that started in the past and is still going on."

⚙️ Morphosyntactic Structure
ACTIVE VOICE
(+) Subject + has/have + been + Verb-ing

"Scientists have been warning us about climate change."

(-) Subject + has/have not + been + Verb-ing

"The government has not been investing enough."

(?) Has/Have + Subject + been + Verb-ing?

"Have you been studying for the IELTS exam?"

PASSIVE VOICE (AVOID THIS!)

Band 8 Critical Rule:

Technically, the passive form is
has been being + V3, but native speakers NEVER use this because it sounds incredibly awkward.

The Band 8 Solution:

Switch back to Present Perfect Simple Passive instead!

"The problem has been discussed for years."
📝 Spelling & Advanced Mechanics
Simple vs. Continuous (The Difference)
Present Perfect SIMPLE:

Focuses on the Result or Completion.

"I have read the book." (I finished it) Present Perfect CONTINUOUS:

Focuses on the Duration or the Activity itself.

"I have been reading the book." (I am still reading it)
Key Academic Markers
Time Durations: For decades | Since the turn of the century | All day | Recently Lately vs. Recently:

Used heavily with this tense to show an action that started not long ago and is continuing.

"The economy has been struggling lately."
🛠️ Core Use Cases (When to use it)
1. Emphasizing Duration

تأکید بر طول مدت یک کار مستمر

To show how long an action has been happening up to now (often with for/since).

"They have been working on this project for 3 years."
"It has been raining since Tuesday."
2. Temporary Ongoing Situations

شرایط موقتی از گذشته تا الان

To talk about a temporary habit or situation that started recently.

"I have been walking to work recently because my car is broken."
"She has been eating healthier lately."
3. Action Finished with Present Evidence

کار تازه تمام شده با اثر فیزیکی مشهود

The action just stopped, but you can see, smell, or feel the immediate result.

"You look exhausted. Have you been running?"
"The ground is wet because it has been raining."
4. Repeated Actions (Persistence)

کارهای تکرارشونده و پافشاری

To express annoyance or emphasis about an action repeated multiple times until now.

"I have been calling you all day! Where were you?"
"Critics have been arguing about this for months."
🎯 IELTS Strategic Application
Writing Task 2 Highlighting Persistent Societal Problems

Use this tense in your body paragraphs to emphasize that a problem is not new, but has been an ongoing struggle for society or governments over a period of time.

Band 8 Example: "For decades, governments have been attempting to eradicate poverty, yet the issue persists."
Speaking Parts 1 & 2 Answering "How long...?" Questions

If the examiner asks "How long have you lived here?" or "How long have you been studying English?", answering with Present Perfect Continuous guarantees a high GRA score.

Band 8 Example: "I have been living in this city since I was a child."
Speaking Part 3 Explaining Current Trends (Lately)

Use this to describe what people in your country are doing *lately* to adapt to new situations.

Band 8 Example: "Recently, more young people have been opting for freelance work rather than traditional office jobs."
🚫 Common Band 6 Pitfalls

Avoid these mistakes to secure a Band 7+ in Grammatical Range and Accuracy (GRA):

  • 1. The Stative Verb Trap (Again!): You CANNOT use verbs of state (know, like, belong, have) in any continuous tense, even this one.
    ❌ I have been knowing my best friend for 10 years.
    ✅ I have known my best friend for 10 years. (Use Present Perfect Simple)
  • 2. Dropping "been": In the heat of the Speaking exam, candidates often mix up tenses and drop the word 'been'.
    ❌ They have working hard lately.
    ✅ They have been working hard lately.
  • 3. Simple vs. Continuous Confusion with Quantities: If you mention exactly "how much" or "how many" things are finished, you MUST use Simple, not Continuous.
    ❌ I have been writing three essays today. (Focus is on the quantity finished)
    ✅ I have written three essays today.
    ✅ I have been writing essays all day. (Focus is on the duration/activity)
